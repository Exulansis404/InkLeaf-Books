Privacy policy

Last updated 12 November 2026

InkLeaf is a book reader and writing app for Android, made by Rashed. In short: your reading and writing stay with you. InkLeaf has no ads, no analytics, no tracking and no InkLeaf servers. The developer never receives your books, notes, writing or account details.

What stays on your phone

Your books, reading positions, highlights, notes, bookmarks, scribbles, stickers, stories, reading statistics and settings are stored inside the app on your device. Usernames and passwords you enter for online bookstores are stored encrypted on your device and are only sent to that bookstore when you sign in to it.

Optional backup to your Google Drive

If you choose Connect Google Drive, InkLeaf asks Google for permission to use only its own private app folder in your Google Drive (the drive.appdata permission). InkLeaf cannot see, open or change any of your other Drive files, and nobody else, including the developer, can see this folder.

The backup can contain your settings, the list of books on your shelf, your reading positions, highlights, notes, bookmarks, scribbles and stickers, your stories and their covers, your reading statistics and the list of bookstores you added (never their passwords). If you turn it on, it also contains copies of book files you added yourself. InkLeaf also reads your Google account's email address and free Drive space, only to show them on the backup screen.

You can delete the backup at any time in InkLeaf (Backup & sync → Delete my backup), or in Google Drive (Settings → Manage apps → InkLeaf → Delete hidden app data). You can remove InkLeaf's access in your Google Account under Security → Third-party apps & services.

InkLeaf's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. This data is used only to back up and restore your InkLeaf data for you. It is not used for advertising, not sold, and not shared with anyone.

Online services InkLeaf talks to

InkLeaf connects directly to these services, only when you use the matching feature. Like any website, they can see your device's internet address and follow their own privacy policies.

  • Free bookstore: Project Gutenberg (gutendex.com, gutenberg.org), Bangla Wikisource (bn.wikisource.org and its export service) and the Internet Archive (archive.org).
  • Bookstores you add or open: for example OPDS catalogs such as Standard Ebooks, Kobo, Smashwords, Google Play Books, Amazon Kindle or Rokomari.
  • Dictionary: when you look up an English word, the word is sent to the Free Dictionary API (dictionaryapi.dev).
  • Google: sign-in and Google Drive, only if you turn on backup.
  • GitHub: to check for and download new versions of InkLeaf.

Read aloud uses your phone's own text-to-speech engine.

Android's own backup

If Android's device backup is turned on in your phone's settings, Android may include InkLeaf's data in your Google device backup. This is handled by Android and Google under their policies.

Children

InkLeaf does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children.
